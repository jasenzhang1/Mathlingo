# Mathlingo

The goal of mathlingo is to be a flashcard and learning platform for everything in college math and beyond that should be drilled and fluent for professionals in ML, AI, quant, and data science.

It should also help college students learn these concepts for the first time. 

Duolingo/Anki but for math and statistics

## Ideas

#### 1. Memory 

Similar to Anki, we wish to use flashcards and vary the frequency of when they are due depending on how well we assess the user to understand the concept.

#### 2. Analogies

We go all-in on our hypothesis that analogies help people teach and learn subjects faster. Most topics will motivate the need for itself with a world life example (i.e. mean and variance of the speed of a tennis player's first serve).

#### 3. Skill Tree

Lots of ideas in calculus, linear algebra, and statistics are borrowed throughout machine learning, finance, and other fields. We hope to visualize a skill-tree of topics that are needed to learn more advanced topics.

#### 4. Critical Thinking

For certain topics, we wish to challenge the learner to think about why certain properties are true. For example, why does the rank of the matrix have to be less than or equal to min(m, n)? 

#### 5. AI scoring

In conjunction with the first idea of memory, an AI grader will assess how much a user knows a topic by how well they answer the open-ended question. The AI grader can grade hand-written answers, voice answers, or typed answers. The AI can even have a conversation with the user to mimic an interview, and give the user a grade on several topics at once.

#### 6. Community Q&A

Learners can submit their own question-and-answer cards to a shared deck. Cards earn their author reputation as the community upvotes them, the strongest cards get pinned to the top of the deck, and milestones — a first card, ten upvotes, cards across three subjects — unlock badges. The best explanation of a concept is usually written by someone who just finished struggling with it.
